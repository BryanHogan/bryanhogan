I've been exploring a better canvas to evaluate business / start-up ideas.

So I've made the Clear Business Idea Canvas, which builds upon the Business Model Canvas and the Lean Canvas.

## Business Model Canvas

There are many ways to plan a business. One of the most well-known ways when starting is the Business Model Canvas (BMC). It describes 9 areas in which you describe your future business. This helps you get a clear overview of what your business will look like and how it will operate. Using such a canvas should help you make better business decisions. It's much faster than writing a whole business plan, and can thus also adapt to any changes faster.

The nine blocks of the Business Model Canvas:

1. Customer Segments
2. Value Propositions
3. Channels
4. Customer Relationships
5. Revenue Streams
6. Key Resources
7. Key Activities
8. Key Partners
9. Cost Structure

<details>
<summary>The complete Business Model Canvas:</summary>

```

## 1. Customer Segments

Who are we creating value for?

- For whom are we creating value?
- Who are our most important customers?
- Examples: Mass Market, Niche Market, Segmented, Diversified, Multi-sided Platform

## 2. Value Propositions

What problems are we solving, and what value do we offer?

- What value do we deliver to the customer?
- Which one of our customer's problems are we helping to solve?
- What bundles of products and services are we offering to each Customer Segment?
- Which customer needs are we satisfying?
- Examples: Newness, Performance, Customization, "Getting the Job Done", Design, Brand/Status, Price, Cost Reduction, Risk Reduction, Accessibility, Convenience/Usability

## 3. Channels

How do we reach our customers?

- Through which Channels do our Customer Segments want to be reached?
- How are we reaching them now?
- How are our Channels integrated?
- Which ones work best?
- Which ones are most cost-efficient?
- How are we integrating them with customer routines?
- Examples: Awareness, Evaluation, Purchase, Delivery, After-Sales (channel phases); Direct Sales, Online Stores, Retail Stores, Distributors, Partner Channels

## 4. Customer Relationships

How do we attract, retain, and support customers?

- What type of relationship does each of our Customer Segments expect us to establish and maintain with them?
- Which ones have we established?
- How are they integrated with the rest of our business model?
- How costly are they?
- Examples: Personal Assistance, Dedicated Personal Assistance, Self-Service, Automated Services, Communities, Co-creation

## 5. Revenue Streams

How will we generate revenue?

- For what value are our customers really willing to pay?
- For what do they currently pay?
- How are they currently paying?
- How would they prefer to pay?
- How much does each Revenue Stream contribute to overall revenues?
- Examples:
	- Types: Asset Sale, Usage Fee, Subscription Fees, Lending/Renting/Leasing, Licensing, Brokerage Fees, Advertising
	- Fixed Pricing: List Price, Product Feature Dependent, Customer Segment Dependent, Volume Dependent
	- Dynamic Pricing: Negotiation (Bargaining), Yield Management, Real-time Market

## 6. Key Resources

What resources do we need to deliver our value proposition?

- What Key Resources do our Value Propositions require?
- Our Distribution Channels?
- Customer Relationships?
- Revenue Streams?
- Examples: Physical (buildings, equipment, inventory), Intellectual (brands, patents, copyrights, data), Human (employees, expertise), Financial (cash, credit, investments)

## 7. Key Activities

What must we do to make the business work?

- What Key Activities do our Value Propositions require?
- Our Distribution Channels?
- Customer Relationships?
- Revenue Streams?
- Examples: Production (manufacturing, product development), Problem Solving (consulting, customer support), Platform/Network (software development, platform maintenance)

## 8. Key Partners

Who do we need to work with?

- Who are our Key Partners?
- Who are our key suppliers?
- Which Key Resources are we acquiring from partners?
- Which Key Activities do partners perform?
- Examples: Strategic Alliances, Suppliers, Joint Ventures, Distribution Partners, Technology Partners
	- Motivations for Partnerships: Optimization and Economy, Reduction of Risk and Uncertainty, Acquisition of Particular Resources and Activities

## 9. Cost Structure

What will it cost to operate the business?

- What are the most important costs inherent in our business model?
- Which Key Resources are most expensive?
- Which Key Activities are most expensive?
- Examples: Leaning towards Cost Driven (leanest cost structure, low price value proposition, maximum automation, extensive outsourcing) or Value Driven (focused on value creation, premium value proposition)?
	- Sample characteristics: Fixed Costs (salaries, rents, utilities), Variable costs, Economies of scale, Economies of scope

```

</details>

## Lean Canvas

The Lean Canvas is an adaptation of the BMC. It also has nine blocks, but instead focuses more on early-stage start-ups.

The nine blocks of the Lean Canvas:

1. Problem
2. Customer Segments
3. Unique Value Proposition
4. Solution
5. Channels
6. Revenue Streams
7. Cost Structure
8. Key Metrics
9. Unfair Advantage

<details>
<summary>The complete Lean Canvas:</summary>

```

## 1. Problem

- List your top 1–3 problems.
- Existing Alternatives: List how these problems are solved today.

## 2. Customer Segments

- List your target customers and users.
- Early Adopters: List the characteristics of your ideal customers.

## 3. Unique Value Proposition

- Single, clear, compelling message that states why you are different and worth paying attention.
- High-Level Concept: List your X for Y analogy e.g. YouTube = Flickr for videos.

## 4. Solution

- Outline a possible solution for each problem.

## 5. Channels

- List your path to customers (inbound or outbound).

## 6. Revenue Streams

- List your sources of revenue.

## 7. Cost Structure

- List your fixed and variable costs.

## 8. Key Metrics

- List the key numbers that tell you how your business is doing.

## 9. Unfair Advantage

- Something that cannot easily be bought or copied.

```

</details>

## Clear Business Idea Canvas

With the Clear Business Idea Canvas I also focus on start-ups and early businesses, but adjust some of the parts that I felt were weaker in the Lean Canvas.

I adjusted two blocks, removed one, and added two new ones compared to the Lean Canvas. The changes:

- Solution → Value Proposition
- Unique Value Proposition → Unique Selling Point
- Unfair Advantage → Founder Fit
- Added: Timing

I prefer focusing on the value proposition, as done in the BMC: what values / features are provided to the customer? Another section already goes into the different customer segments and their problems and needs. So here we can map the connections between pain and need within the customer and the value proposition and features of the product.

With that background we do not need to focus on just the unique value proposition, but instead on the unique selling point: what makes the product different from other offers and processes.

These two areas already explore which potential advantages there are, especially the unique selling point. Going into unfair advantages felt meaningless. I found especially the "unfair" part to be the weakest part of the Lean Canvas, as it doesn't matter if an advantage is unfair or not.

One major factor for many successful start-ups was their timing: when did they enter the market? This may look at trends and changes in the potential users and potential customers, but also at how processes have changed and will change in the future to create such a product.

The final, tenth block is Founder Fit. The founders are the core of the start-up and of every decision in the beginning, so including them seems right.

The 10 blocks of the Clear Business Idea Canvas:

1. Problem
2. Customer Segments
3. Value Proposition
4. Unique Selling Point
5. Channels
6. Revenue Streams
7. Cost Structure
8. Key Metrics
9. Timing
10. Founder Fit

<summary>The complete Clear Business Idea Canvas:</summary>

```
## 1. Problem

- List your top problems.
- Existing Alternatives: List how these problems are solved today.

## 2. Customer Segments

- List your target customers and users.
- Early Adopters: List the characteristics of your ideal customers.

## 3. Value Proposition

- What problems are you solving, and what value do you offer?
- What value do you deliver to the customer?
- Which of your customers' problems are you helping to solve?
- What bundles of products and services are you offering to each Customer Segment?
- Which customer needs are you satisfying?

## 4. Unique Selling Point

- Why and how are you different from competitors / existing solutions?
- How are competitors positioned? Have they demonstrated an existing market need?

## 5. Channels

- List your path to customers (inbound or outbound).

## 6. Revenue Streams

- List your sources of revenue.

## 7. Cost Structure

- List your fixed and variable costs.

## 8. Key Metrics

- List the key numbers that tell you how your business is doing.

## 9. Timing

- Why is now a good time to start / launch?
- Why is now a bad time to start / launch?

## 10. Founder Fit

- What relevant knowledge, skills, experience or connections do you / the founding team have?
- What are you / the founding team lacking?
- What motivates you to pursue this idea?

```
